Research software · not a financial firm

A notebook for probability sketches on closed bars.

MQuant is a research interface. It draws an ensemble of hypothetical paths, a few diagnostics, and — only sometimes — a level sketch on the last closed bar. It does not place orders, hold client money, or tell anyone what to buy or sell.

What this is

Software, not a broker

MQuant is research software sold as a monthly subscription. No financial-services license is claimed, no client money is held, and no orders are placed.

Notes, not instructions

A pass can end as a note, a watch, or silence. Silence is a normal result when the notebook does not see a sketch worth writing down.

Closed bars, not a live book

Sketches refer to the last closed bar. They are not a quote from an order book and they are not an instruction to enter a market.

You decide

The interface does not execute trades and does not promise a profit, a durable advantage, or a particular outcome.

How a pass is organized

  1. Instrument. A ticker, a horizon, and a path count are chosen in the sidebar of the notebook.
  2. One pass. The ensemble draws paths, levels, and diagnostics together.
  3. Read the spread. Path probabilities sit next to a sketch of hypothetical levels on the last closed bar.
  4. After the bar closes. A note is attached to that closed bar. It is not a live entry.

What is inside

Overview

One reading, a score, and the thresholds that separate a note from silence.

Path cone

A spread of outcomes from a mixture of models, not a single assumed path.

Level sketch

Hypothetical levels on a closed bar, drawn only when a note is published. Not a quotation and not an order.

Path summary

A model average across simulated paths. Labeled as a calculation, not as a forecast of profit.

News window

A high-impact news window can withdraw a note before the event.

History check

The same rules can be read back over past bars before anyone risks their own capital.

Volume context

High- and low-volume nodes as background for levels, not as a command.

Sessions and neighbors

Asia, London, and New York as session context, plus related instruments so one ticker is not read alone.

How it differs from a trading desk

  • Several simple models are mixed. An average of past bars is not treated as if it were the whole distribution.
  • The notebook may publish nothing. That is a valid output.
  • Levels and path probabilities are shown together, on a closed bar.
  • Calibration notes (including a Kolmogorov–Smirnov check and a deflated Sharpe figure, on plans that include them) are visible before a note is written.
  • Where a public quote is shown, it may be delayed. The page does not claim a direct exchange feed.
